# The content fuel system

The engine's job is to make sure you never start from a blank page, and never publish something anyone could have written.

## 1. Five input streams

| Stream | Where it comes from | What it gives you |
|---|---|---|
| **News** | The scanner (`scanner/sources/digests/`) | What changed this week, with a source card: summary, numbers, a quote, the date |
| **Your data** | Search Console + GA4 (`analytics/opportunities.json`) | Queries you already show up for, pages to fix, questions people ask, AI-assistant visits |
| **Your site's health** | The weekly audit (`seo-audit/reports/latest.md`) | What's holding every page back |
| **Your work** | You — one line a day in Notion or a note | The moment no one else has: a result, a mistake, a client question (anonymized) |
| **Your audience** | Comments, DMs, sales/support questions | The exact words people use |

## 2. The anchor rule

Every piece of content needs **2 of 3**:
1. **A source** — dated, linked, with a concrete fact, number or quote
2. **A work moment** — something you saw in your work, anonymized
3. **An artifact** — a test you ran, a screenshot, a before/after, or a real number from your own data

**The generic test:** could anyone in your niche have written this without reading this week's news or doing your job? If yes, it isn't ready.

**Never invent a number**, not even as an illustration.

## 3. The 3-filter

Every fuel item gets one point for each:
1. **In your pillars, and the audience cares** — it changes something they do. (A match with a query in your own Search Console data earns the point automatically.)
2. **You have an angle** — your lens, your data, your experience; not a summary.
3. **It fits in one sharp line** — if you can't write the hook, it isn't ready.

3/3 → write it this week · 2/3 → park it and note what's missing · 1/3 → archive. News older than 5 days drops a level.

## 4. How the skills use it

| Skill | Reads | Makes |
|---|---|---|
| `topic-list` | Digest + opportunities | Today's ranked topics |
| `weekly-plan` | Digests, topic lists, analytics, audit, your notes | The week, scored and scheduled |
| `blog-post` | A topic + sources + your data | The canonical post (Deep or Decode) |
| `news-fast-lane` | One news item | Decode post, Reel script, quote card, captions |
| `blog-cascade` | A finished deep post | LinkedIn ×2, newsletter section, carousel, Reel, quote card |
| `linkedin-posts`, `medium-article`, `reel` | The topic or post | That format, done properly |
| `search-analytics` | Analytics + audit | This week's 5 actions |
| `seo-audit` | The crawl (+ OpenSEO) | 1–3 fixes worth the effort |

## 5. One topic, many formats

A topic runs as a series: the blog post is the most complete version; every other format covers a **different detail** (the surprising number, the risk, what to do, the contrarian take, the regional angle) and points back to the post. Never post the same point twice, and never make one format a summary of another.
